# Opita Sync Commercial and Pricing Analysis

## What The Product Is

Opita Sync is not just a chatbot or a helpdesk. It is a governed operational layer that connects conversational intake, case execution, operational logging, assisted drafting, auditability, tenant configuration, and controlled AI/tool usage inside one product model.

In practical terms, the product does four things together:

1. Receives operational intent in natural language.
2. Resolves that intent against governed capabilities, roles, tools, and policy modes.
3. Converts the interaction into auditable operational work around cases, logs, drafts, and value events.
4. Gives Opita and the tenant enough governance to scale usage without losing control.

## How The Product Works

Operationally, the system starts in chat, but does not end there. The request is resolved using tenant, role, case, and capability context. The product then decides whether to suggest, request confirmation, or execute. Every meaningful action creates case history, audit evidence, and attributable value or usage records.

This means the product sells better as a controlled operations copilot than as a generic AI assistant. Its value comes from reducing operational friction while preserving governance, traceability, and tenant-specific process fit.

## How The Product Should Be Sold

The right commercial framing is service-operations transformation, not token resale.

The sales narrative should be:

- faster operational response from chat to case execution;
- governed AI usage instead of unbounded chat behavior;
- mobile-first operational experience for field and frontline roles;
- auditable actions for supervisors and regulated environments;
- configurable tenant behavior without custom software on day one;
- measurable business value through adoption, positive actions, and operational speed.

That positioning supports selling by platform value and operational scope, not by raw model consumption.

## Billing Decision

Billing and invoicing stay outside the platform. Inside Opita Sync, the product should only keep:

- attributable usage by tenant;
- successful action counts;
- positive action evidence;
- exportable metering records for finance or external invoicing workflows.

This is strategically cleaner because the product remains focused on operations and governance, while commercial operations can evolve outside the app.

## Commercial Reading Of The Market

The market shows three recurring patterns:

1. Base platform pricing is usually per agent or per seat.
2. Advanced AI is often sold either as a premium edition, an add-on, or a usage-based layer.
3. Enterprise-grade automation and governance are frequently sold through custom pricing tied to ROI and operational scale.

Opita Sync should not compete as the cheapest ticketing tool. It should compete as a higher-value operational governance layer with AI-native workflows. That places it closer to the upper-middle and enterprise segment than to low-cost helpdesk software.

## Recommended Pricing Model For Opita Sync

Use external commercial packaging with three layers:

1. One-time onboarding and enablement fee
2. Monthly platform subscription by tenant and scope
3. Optional expansion fees for added capabilities, integrations, or premium operational support

Avoid a pricing model based only on tokens, prompts, or raw model calls. That weakens value perception and commoditizes the offer.

## Suggested Prices

### Pilot

- Suggested price: `USD 2,500/month`
- One-time onboarding: `USD 4,000`
- Target: first tenant, one operational area, up to 25 active operational users
- Includes: chat, cases, logbook, audit baseline, mobile-first core flows, basic reporting, certified capability pack
- Sales use: pilot to prove adoption, speed, and operational control in 8 to 12 weeks

### Growth

- Suggested price: `USD 6,000/month`
- One-time onboarding: `USD 8,000`
- Target: multi-team tenant, up to 100 active users
- Includes: pilot scope plus advanced role controls, tenant parameterization, assisted drafting, value reporting, external billing exports, additional certified capability packs
- Sales use: customers that already validated one operational use case and need rollout to more teams

### Enterprise

- Suggested price: `USD 12,000 to 20,000/month`
- One-time onboarding: `USD 12,000 to 25,000`
- Target: larger tenants, multi-area rollout, stronger governance, compliance, and custom operating model requirements
- Includes: growth scope plus premium governance, multi-domain rollout, advanced support, tailored compliance posture, executive reporting, and prioritized roadmap collaboration
- Sales use: regulated or complex organizations where control and operating fit matter more than simple seat economics

## Suggested Expansion Prices

- Additional certified capability pack: `USD 750 to 1,500/month`
- Additional tenant environment or business unit scope: `USD 1,000 to 2,500/month`
- Premium operational support / success cadence: `USD 1,500 to 4,000/month`
- Complex integration or migration project: scoped separately as professional services

## Why These Prices Fit

These prices are above commodity helpdesk entry pricing and below the perceived cost of deploying a full enterprise transformation stack with heavy custom consulting. That is the correct middle ground for a product that combines:

- mobile-first operations;
- governed AI execution;
- tenant configuration;
- auditability;
- measurable adoption and value visibility;
- externalized billing with internal metering.

In other words, Opita Sync should be sold on business control and operational throughput, not on cheap seats.

## Recommended Sales Motion

- Sell the Pilot first.
- Use pilot proof points from adoption, cycle-time reduction, blocked-risk avoidance, and positive actions.
- Expand to Growth when one area reaches stable weekly usage and measurable operational lift.
- Move to Enterprise only when governance, rollout breadth, or compliance complexity justify it.

## Recommended Internal Pricing Rule

Use these suggested prices as the default list-price baseline in proposals until real sales evidence forces adjustment. Discounting should happen through pilot duration, onboarding concessions, or bundled capability packs, not by collapsing the monthly platform price too early.
